# 1. Program Overview

## 1.1 Purpose

This plan sets out a role-based Security Awareness Program for NexusGuard Technologies Ltd. ("NexusGuard") for the 2026 fiscal year. Its purpose is to change what people do, not just what they know: fewer clicks on phishing, fewer stolen credentials, faster reporting, and careful handling of the PHI, payment card data and personal data that clients trust NexusGuard with.

## 1.2 Why Now

- **The simulation results are poor.** In the recent internal phishing simulation, **34%** of employees clicked the link and **18%** went on to enter their credentials. A stolen password is often all an attacker needs.
- **Training has lapsed.** The last awareness training was **22 months ago** and used generic, outdated slides. It did not cover cloud tools, remote work, AI tools or the current phishing tricks.
- **The stakes are high.** NexusGuard handles regulated data under HIPAA, PCI DSS and GDPR, and clients and auditors expect a documented, current program.
- **The rules have changed.** New policies on acceptable use, access control and data classification need to be explained so that staff can follow them.

## 1.3 Scope and Audience

The program covers all 450 employees, plus contractors and temporary staff with access to NexusGuard systems, in four segments: General Staff (320), IT and Technical Staff (65), Management and Executives (35) and Customer-Facing Teams (30). Every employee takes the core modules, and each specialist segment adds its own track.

# 3. Target Audience Analysis

The four segments differ in size, in the data they touch and in the attacks aimed at them, so one course cannot fit all of them. The 34% and 18% results are organization-wide. Segment-level results shall be taken from the simulation platform in the first month, to confirm the priorities below.

**Table 2: Audience Segments**

| Segment | Size | Data and systems touched | Main threats | Learning needs | Best approach |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| General Staff | 320 (71%) | Email, shared files, internal systems, some Confidential data | Phishing, credential theft, weak passwords, tailgating, unsafe remote work | Basic habits: spot, stop, report; passwords and MFA; physical security | Short e-learning, frequent micro-lessons, simulations, campaigns |
| IT and Technical Staff | 65 (14%) | Source code, servers, cloud, privileged accounts, production access | Credential theft for admin access, secrets in code, unpatched systems, insecure configuration | Secure coding, vulnerability management, incident response, privileged access hygiene | Hands-on workshops, labs, tabletop exercises |
| Management and Executives | 35 (8%) | Strategy, financial approvals, regulated data decisions | Business email compromise, whaling, impersonation, regulatory exposure | BEC and social engineering, data governance, regulatory duties | Short live briefings, executive tabletop, quarterly updates |
| Customer-Facing Teams | 30 (7%) | Client PHI and personal data, support systems, customer contacts | Pretexting and vishing from callers, data requests, mishandling of PHI | Data handling, HIPAA and GDPR, verifying who is asking | Role-play, case studies, call-back procedure practice |

**3.1** **Priority.** General Staff are the largest group and the main source of the click rate, so they get the most frequent contact. Management and Customer-Facing Teams are small but face targeted attacks with high impact, so they get tailored sessions. IT staff hold the most powerful access, so a compromised IT account does the most damage, and they get the deepest technical training.

**3.2** **Constraints.** Staff have limited time, so core modules are short (10 to 45 minutes). Hybrid and travelling staff need content that works on any device. Language versions shall be agreed with HR.

## 5.1 Phishing and Vishing Simulation Program

**5.1.1** Simulations shall run every second month (February, April, June, August, October and December) for all staff, which exceeds the quarterly minimum in the Information Security Policy. Difficulty rises in three levels, from obvious to well-crafted, and themes follow real threats: credential harvesting, business email compromise, MFA fatigue, QR codes, and delivery and invoice scams.

**5.1.2** Anyone who clicks sees a short teaching page and a 5-minute lesson. The response is supportive, not punitive. Staff who click in two or more rounds receive one-to-one coaching, and their manager is told. Staff who click in three or more rounds complete extra training and are reviewed for added technical safeguards.

**5.1.3** Simulations shall be approved in advance by HR and Legal, shall not use sensitive pretexts (pay, redundancy, health or bereavement), and results shall be used only for training and program measurement, not for individual performance rating.

**5.1.4** The customer-facing track includes a vishing test each year in which a trusted tester tries to extract information from staff, with immediate coaching.

# 6. Completion Tracking and Enforcement

**6.1** HR shall assign every module in the LMS with a due date, and the LMS shall record completion, quiz scores and attempt history for each person. Records shall be kept for six years.

**6.2** New joiners shall complete the core security essentials within 30 days of starting, and before they are given access to Confidential or Restricted systems.

# 7. KPIs and Measurement Framework

Measurement works at four levels: reach (did people take part), learning (did they learn), behavior (did they act differently) and outcomes (did risk fall). The behavior measures matter most.

**Table 6: Program KPIs**

| KPI | Formula | Source | Frequency | Baseline | June | December |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phishing click rate | Staff who clicked / staff who received | Simulation platform | Every 2 months | 34% | 14% | 5% |
| Credential entry rate | Staff who entered credentials / staff who received | Simulation platform | Every 2 months | 18% | 5% | 1% |
| Phishing report rate | Staff who reported / staff who received | Simulation platform; SOC | Every 2 months | Not measured | 45% | 70% |
| Median time to report | Median minutes from delivery to report | Simulation platform | Every 2 months | Not measured | 60 min | 30 min |
| Repeat clickers | Staff who clicked in 2 or more rounds / all staff | Simulation platform | Every 2 months | Not measured | 7% | 2% |
| Training completed on time | Assigned modules done by due date / assigned | LMS | Monthly | No program | 95% | 98% |
| Average quiz score | Mean score of quizzes taken in the period | LMS | Monthly | Not measured | 85% | 85% |
| New joiners trained in 30 days | Joiners trained in 30 days / joiners | LMS; HR | Monthly | Not measured | 100% | 100% |
| Security culture score | Positive answers in the staff survey | Survey (January and December) | Twice a year | Set in January | n/a | +20 points |
| Human-error incidents | Incidents with human error as cause | Incident register | Quarterly | To extract | Falling | 30% fewer than prior year |

**7.1** Status uses red, amber and green. For measures where lower is better, green is at or below target, amber up to 25% above and red above that. Where higher is better, green is at or above target, amber down to 90% of target and red below. The KPI Tracker in the calendar workbook applies these rules.

**7.2** The program shall be reviewed after each simulation round and each quarter. Content shall be updated when incidents, simulation results or new threats show a gap, and the annual review shall record the changes (PCI DSS 12.6.2).

**7.3** Awareness metrics feed the GRC KPI and Metrics Framework, including MFA coverage, privileged access and incident reporting, so that training results are read alongside control results.

# 11. Risks and Dependencies

| Risk or dependency | Effect | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Low engagement or training fatigue | Completion and learning fall short | Keep modules short, vary formats, use champions and recognition |
| Simulations seen as a trap | Distrust; less reporting | Explain the purpose; supportive response; no sensitive pretexts; HR and Legal approval |
| LMS or platform delays | Launch slips | Confirm LMS capacity and order the simulation platform in the first month |
| Content goes stale | Training loses relevance | Refresh after each round and on new threats; annual review |
| Managers do not release staff | Late completion | Escalation in Table 5; manager reporting; CISO support |
| Segment data not available | Targets set blindly | Pull segment results from the platform in month one |
| Weak baseline for several KPIs | Targets not anchored | Measure in the first two rounds, then reset targets once |
