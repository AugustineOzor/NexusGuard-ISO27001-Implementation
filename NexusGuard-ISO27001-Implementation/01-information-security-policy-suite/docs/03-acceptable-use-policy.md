# 1. Purpose

This Acceptable Use Policy (the "Policy") sets out how NexusGuard Technologies Ltd. ("NexusGuard") technology, information and services may and may not be used. It protects NexusGuard, its clients and its people from the risks created by misuse, including data loss, regulatory breach, malware, legal liability and damage to client trust.

NexusGuard handles confidential client information, including protected health information and payment data. The generic IT agreement that staff signed in 2019 does not address cloud services, personal devices or remote work. This Policy replaces it and gives NexusGuard a clear, enforceable basis for setting expectations and for taking disciplinary action where those expectations are not met.

This Policy shall:

- define acceptable use of company-owned devices, personal devices, internet, social media, email, and cloud and SaaS services;
- prohibit specific activities, including cryptocurrency mining, forwarding sensitive data to personal accounts, illegal content and unauthorized software;
- set security requirements for remote work and home networks;
- inform users that their use of NexusGuard systems is monitored and may be audited; and
- state the tiered consequences of violations, from a warning to termination.

# 5. Policy Statements

## 5.1 General Principles

**5.1.1** NexusGuard systems and information shall be used for legitimate business purposes. Limited, occasional personal use is permitted only where it is lawful, does not interfere with work, does not use significant resources, does not breach this Policy, and does not put information at risk.

**5.1.2** Users shall comply with applicable laws, client contracts and NexusGuard policies, and shall act honestly and professionally when using NexusGuard technology.

**5.1.3** Users shall not use NexusGuard systems for personal business, outside employment or any activity for personal financial gain.

**5.1.4** Users who are unsure whether an activity is permitted shall ask their line manager, IT or Security before proceeding.

**5.1.5** This Policy forms part of the terms of employment or engagement. It supersedes the generic IT agreement signed in 2019, and where the two differ this Policy prevails.

**5.1.6** Nothing in this Policy restricts rights protected by law, including the right to report wrongdoing to a regulator or authority, to raise concerns about working conditions where legally protected, or to seek legal advice.

## 5.2 Company-Owned Devices

**5.2.1** Company-owned devices shall be used primarily for NexusGuard business and shall remain the property of NexusGuard. Users shall not lend them to family members, friends or anyone else.

**5.2.2** Devices shall be enrolled in device management and shall run the approved encryption, endpoint protection, firewall and automatic update settings. Users shall not disable, remove or bypass these controls.

**5.2.3** Screens shall lock automatically after no more than 10 minutes of inactivity, and users shall lock them manually whenever they leave a device unattended.

**5.2.4** Users shall protect devices from loss and theft, shall not leave them unattended in vehicles or public places, and shall report a lost or stolen device immediately and within 1 hour of discovery.

**5.2.5** Phones and tablets shall use a passcode or biometric lock, shall not be jailbroken or rooted, and shall install applications only from approved stores and sources.

**5.2.6** Users shall not install software on company devices except from the approved catalog, and shall not change administrator or system settings unless authorized.

**5.2.7** Users shall return all company-owned devices, accessories and data on request, and within 24 hours of the end of their employment or engagement. NexusGuard may remotely lock or wipe a device that is lost, stolen or not returned.

## 5.3 Personal Devices (BYOD)

**5.3.1** Personal devices may access NexusGuard email and applications only after the user has requested and received approval and the device is enrolled in the managed container. Unenrolled personal devices shall not be used for work.

**5.3.2** Personal devices shall run a supported operating system with current security updates, shall use a passcode or biometric lock and device encryption, and shall not be jailbroken or rooted.

**5.3.3** Work data shall be held only inside the managed container. Users shall not copy work data to personal apps, personal cloud storage, local folders or other devices.

**5.3.4** Restricted information, including PHI and cardholder data, shall not be stored or processed on personal devices.

**5.3.5** Personal devices shall connect to NexusGuard systems only through approved secure access (VPN or zero-trust access with MFA) or the BYOD network segment, and never to the internal corporate network directly.

**5.3.6** Users shall not allow family members or others to use a device or session that has access to NexusGuard data, and shall report loss or theft of the device within 1 hour of discovery.

**5.3.7** NexusGuard may remove the managed container and its data remotely when a user leaves, when the device is lost or stolen, or when the device becomes non-compliant. NexusGuard shall not access personal photos, messages or files on a personal device, and a full device wipe shall be carried out only with the owner's consent given at enrollment or where the law permits.

**5.3.8** Users are responsible for their personal devices, personal data and backups. NexusGuard is not liable for loss of personal data that results from compliance with this Policy.

## 5.4 Internet and Social Media

**5.4.1** Internet access on NexusGuard networks and devices shall be filtered. Users shall not attempt to bypass filtering, use anonymizing proxies or unapproved VPNs, or use peer-to-peer or torrent software.

**5.4.2** Reasonable personal browsing is permitted outside focused work time, provided it complies with this Policy, does not use excessive bandwidth (including streaming and large downloads), and does not involve prohibited content.

**5.4.3** On personal social media accounts, users shall not disclose sensitive information, client identities, unreleased products, security incidents or internal systems details, and shall not post images of screens, badges, whiteboards or restricted areas.

**5.4.4** Users shall not speak on behalf of NexusGuard on social media unless authorized by Marketing and Communications. Users who mention their employer shall make clear that views are personal. Official accounts shall be limited to approved roles and protected with MFA.

## 5.5 Email and Messaging

**5.5.1** NexusGuard email and approved messaging tools shall be used for business communications and are NexusGuard records.

**5.5.2** Users shall not forward, send, copy or upload NexusGuard or client information to personal email accounts (including Gmail, Outlook.com and Yahoo), personal messaging apps or personal cloud storage, for any reason, including convenience or working from home.

**5.5.3** Automatic forwarding of NexusGuard email to any external address is prohibited and shall be blocked by technical controls.

**5.5.4** Sensitive information shall be sent only to authorized recipients, through approved encrypted channels, with the minimum data needed. Users shall check the recipient address and attachments before sending.

**5.5.5** Data loss prevention controls shall scan outbound email and file transfers. Users shall not attempt to evade them, for example by renaming, compressing, encrypting or splitting files.

**5.5.6** Users who send information to the wrong recipient, or who receive it in error, shall report it immediately and within 1 hour of discovery. Prompt, honest reporting is a mitigating factor under Section 7.

**5.5.7** Users shall report suspected phishing through the designated reporting button or channel and shall not click links or open attachments they do not trust.

## 5.6 Cloud Services, SaaS and Shadow IT

**5.6.1** Only cloud services, SaaS tools and AI tools listed in the Approved Software and Services Register shall be used for NexusGuard work or with NexusGuard information. Approved services shall be accessed with company accounts, single sign-on and MFA.

**5.6.2** Shadow IT is prohibited. Users shall not sign up for cloud or SaaS services with a NexusGuard email address, or use any unapproved service to store, process or share NexusGuard information.

**5.6.3** A user who needs a new service shall submit a request to IT and Security, who shall assess security, privacy, contract and data location before approval.

**5.6.4** Personal cloud storage (such as personal Dropbox, Google Drive, iCloud or OneDrive) and personal messaging apps shall not be used to store or share NexusGuard information or to communicate with clients about work.

**5.6.5** Generative AI and similar tools shall be used only if approved. Users shall not enter sensitive information, personal data or client data into unapproved tools, and shall check AI output before relying on it.

**5.6.6** Sharing links in approved services shall be limited to named, authorized people. Public or anonymous links shall not be used for Confidential or Restricted information.

**5.6.7** Users who discover or suspect shadow IT, or who have used an unapproved service, shall report it to IT and Security so that data can be recovered and risks managed.

## 5.7 Prohibited Activities

**5.7.1** The activities in Table 1 are prohibited on, or using, NexusGuard devices, networks, accounts and information, and on personal devices that access NexusGuard information.

**Table 1: Prohibited Activities**

| Category | Includes | Why it is prohibited |
|---|---|---|
| Cryptocurrency mining and resource misuse | Installing or running mining software; joining mining pools; cryptojacking; running unauthorized servers, bots or compute-heavy personal workloads; using company cloud accounts or electricity for personal gain | Drains resources, raises costs, creates malware and security exposure |
| Illegal or offensive content | Accessing, storing or sharing unlawful material, child sexual abuse material, terrorism or extremist content, hate speech, harassment, sexually explicit or gambling content | Unlawful, harmful and a breach of conduct standards |
| Unauthorized software | Pirated or cracked software; unapproved remote-access, file-sharing or torrent tools; unlicensed applications; cryptocurrency wallets or trading apps without an approved business need | Licensing breach and malware risk |
| Data exfiltration | Forwarding or uploading sensitive data to personal email or cloud; copying to USB or personal devices; photographing screens; printing sensitive data for personal use | Loss of client data and regulatory breach |
| Circumventing security | Disabling endpoint protection, encryption or logging; bypassing web filters or DLP; using unapproved VPNs; port scanning, vulnerability testing or password cracking without written authorization | Defeats controls that protect NexusGuard and clients |
| Credential and access abuse | Sharing passwords or tokens; using another person's account; accessing systems or data without a business need; attempting to gain higher access | Undermines accountability and least privilege |
| Malicious activity | Creating, spreading or deliberately running malware; sabotage; denial-of-service activity | Direct harm to systems and clients |
| Intellectual property and misuse of client data | Copying or sharing copyrighted material without permission; using client data for non-work purposes; taking NexusGuard source code or documents when leaving | Legal liability and client breach |

**5.7.2** Authorized Security Operations staff may carry out an otherwise prohibited activity (for example scanning or testing) only for a defined purpose, with written authorization from the CISO.

**5.7.3** Users who are asked to carry out a prohibited activity by anyone, including a manager, shall refuse and report the request.

## 5.8 Remote Work and Home Network Security

**5.8.1** Remote work shall be approved by the line manager, shall be done from a country approved by NexusGuard, and shall use company-owned or enrolled devices only. Work from abroad shall be approved in advance by HR and Security.

**5.8.2** Users shall connect to NexusGuard systems through the approved VPN or zero-trust access with MFA. Public or untrusted Wi-Fi shall be used only with the VPN active.

**5.8.3** Home Wi-Fi shall use WPA2 or WPA3 encryption with a unique passphrase of at least 12 characters. Users shall change the default router administrator password and install router updates.

**5.8.4** Users shall work where screens and calls cannot be seen or overheard, shall lock devices when away, and shall keep company devices and papers locked away when not in use.

**5.8.5** Confidential and Restricted information shall not be printed at home. Any paper that must be held shall be locked away and securely destroyed.

**5.8.6** Remote workers shall complete a home-working security self-assessment when they start remote work and each year, and shall report any compromise of their home network.

## 5.9 Protection of Information

**5.9.1** Users shall handle information according to its classification under the Data Classification and Handling Policy, shall store it only in approved locations, and shall access only the information their role requires.

## 5.10 Monitoring, Audit and Privacy

**5.10.1** NexusGuard monitors the use of its systems. Users shall have no expectation of privacy in their use of NexusGuard devices, networks, email, accounts, applications and information, and acknowledge this in Appendix A.

**5.10.2** Monitoring may include email and message content and metadata, web and network traffic, file transfers, application and cloud activity, device and security logs, data loss prevention alerts, and physical access and video surveillance records.

**5.10.3** Monitoring is carried out for security, compliance with legal and client obligations, detecting misuse, investigating incidents, and protecting NexusGuard and client information. It shall be proportionate, limited to what is necessary, and carried out under a documented legal basis and privacy impact assessment.

**5.10.4** Monitoring records shall be accessible only to authorized Security, HR and Legal personnel. Access to an individual's records shall require written approval from the CISO and the HR Director, with Legal advice.

**5.10.5** Reviewers shall make reasonable efforts to avoid opening items clearly marked personal, and shall not review them unless a serious breach or legal duty requires it and Legal has approved.

**5.10.6** Covert monitoring is prohibited unless Legal, HR and the DPO approve it in writing for a suspected serious breach, and it shall be time-limited.

**5.10.7** NexusGuard may audit compliance with this Policy, including inspecting and forensically imaging company-owned devices. For personal devices, NexusGuard shall limit audit to the managed container and compliance status unless the user consents or the law requires otherwise.

**5.10.8** Users shall cooperate with investigations and audits, including by providing access to company devices and accounts, and shall not delete, alter or conceal evidence.

**5.10.9** Monitoring records shall be retained for 12 months unless an investigation or legal hold requires longer. Users may exercise their data protection rights, including access, through the DPO.

**5.10.10** NexusGuard shall give each user written notice of monitoring and obtain their acknowledgment on joining and annually. Where local law requires a posted or specific notice (for example in New York, the UK or the UAE), that notice shall also be given.

## 5.11 Reporting Violations and Incidents

**5.11.1** Users shall report suspected violations of this Policy, lost devices, phishing, misdirected information and other incidents immediately, and within 1 hour of discovery, through the designated incident reporting channel or to their line manager, HR or Security.

**5.11.2** Users who suspect serious wrongdoing may report it confidentially to HR, Legal or the CISO, or through any whistleblowing channel NexusGuard provides.

**5.11.3** NexusGuard shall not retaliate against anyone who reports a concern in good faith. Retaliation is itself a violation of this Policy.

# 7. Enforcement and Sanctions

**7.1** Compliance with this Policy is mandatory. A violation may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment or engagement, in addition to any civil, criminal or regulatory consequences.

**7.2** Disciplinary action shall follow the HR disciplinary procedure and the sanction policy required under HIPAA 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(C), and shall comply with applicable employment law in each jurisdiction and with the user's contract.

**7.3** Violations shall be assessed using the tiers in Table 3. The tiers are a guide. The sanction applied shall reflect intent, harm or risk caused, the sensitivity of the data, the user's role, prior record, and cooperation and prompt self-reporting.

**Table 3: Tiered Sanctions**

| Tier | Typical violations | Typical sanction |
|---|---|---|
| 1: Minor | First, unintentional breach with no harm: leaving a screen unlocked, minor excess personal use, a late incident report | Verbal warning, coaching and retraining |
| 2: Moderate | Repeated Tier 1 breaches; installing unapproved software; using an unapproved SaaS tool for non-sensitive data; connecting an unenrolled personal device; posting internal information on social media; breaching home-working rules | Written warning, mandatory training, and removal of remote work or BYOD access until compliant |
| 3: Serious | Deliberately disabling or bypassing controls; sharing credentials; storing sensitive data in personal cloud or unapproved services; accessing offensive content; using company resources for personal gain, including cryptocurrency mining (depending on extent and intent) | Final written warning, suspension (paid or unpaid as the law and contract allow), demotion or loss of privileges, and recovery of costs where lawful |
| 4: Gross misconduct | Forwarding or copying Confidential or Restricted client data to personal accounts; illegal content; malicious activity; theft or sale of data; concealing or tampering with evidence; repeated Tier 3 breaches; large-scale or deliberate cryptocurrency mining | Termination, including summary dismissal where the law permits; referral to law enforcement and regulators; civil recovery of losses |

**7.4** NexusGuard may move directly to any tier, including termination, where the conduct warrants it. Progressive discipline is not a precondition to action for serious breaches.

**7.5** Where a violation involves sensitive data, Security and the DPO shall assess whether it is a reportable incident under Section 5.12 of the Information Security Policy, and regulatory and client notification shall proceed regardless of any disciplinary outcome.

**7.6** Investigations shall follow these steps: report and triage; preservation of evidence with chain of custody; investigation by HR and Security with Legal; written notice to the user of the allegation and a fair opportunity to respond; a reasoned decision by HR with the user's line manager and Legal; and a right of appeal under the HR procedure.

**7.7** Access to systems may be suspended immediately, pending investigation, where there is a risk to data or systems. Suspension of access is not a sanction.

**7.8** Contractors and temporary staff who breach this Policy shall have access removed and their contract terminated, and third parties shall be subject to the remedies in their contracts. NexusGuard may recover losses and seek legal remedies.

**7.9** Line managers who knowingly allow, instruct or fail to act on violations are themselves subject to disciplinary action.

**7.10** HR shall record sanctions and apply them consistently across all offices and entities, including PayStream Analytics.

# 8. Exceptions

**8.1** Exceptions to this Policy shall be requested through the Policy Exception Management Process and shall be formally requested in writing, with a business justification, a risk assessment and compensating controls.

**8.2** Exceptions shall be approved by the CISO for Low and Medium risk and by the Executive Leadership Team for High or Critical risk. They shall be time-limited to no more than 12 months, recorded in the exception register and reviewed before expiry.

**8.3** No exception shall be granted for a requirement imposed by law or a client contract unless Legal confirms in writing that it is permitted.

**8.4** An activity carried out without an approved exception remains a violation under Section 7.
